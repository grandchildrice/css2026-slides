# Presentation conventions

- Treat `slides.md` as the presentation and speaker-note source; `index.html` renders it using Reveal.js 5.1.0 Markdown. `css2026-slides.json` is the slide inventory, not a content generator.
- Audience: general engineers who understand implementation and testing, but may not know Ethereum, consensus, or formal verification. Do not infer audience expertise from presenters, affiliations, or workshop tracks. Explain Ethereum and multi-implementation systems before client names and finality.
- Central contribution: extract preconditions from safety proofs and supply them as candidate review items; do not turn the talk into a tutorial about Ethereum finality, Casper FFG, quorum arithmetic, or local/global rules. State the proposal on the cover, establish the shared-spec/multiple-implementation context, motivate missing precondition coverage with a clearly labelled scenario, then show extraction, artifacts, evaluation, and practical use. Definitions are only added when needed to follow that argument.
- Structure: concrete example, research finding, general engineering implication, actionable next steps. The main deck is a ~15-minute cut (14 main slides + 14 appendix slides) for a 20-minute slot with 5 minutes of Q&A. Separate main messages from supporting evidence; vary diagrams, scenarios, charts, quotations, and code comparisons instead of repeating bullet-list layouts.
- The main deck follows the standard research skeleton 背景 → 従来の問題点 → 仮説 → この研究の優位性, announced on the cover and shown as `.section-tag` pills above each slide title (section assignments are recorded in `css2026-slides.json` `structure`). The incident example is only one application instance (適用の一例), not the thesis.
- Introduction: title the background slide `イーサリアムについて`; describe third-party operation briefly and show the official color logo. Do not restore the redundant one-computer-versus-network takeaway box. Display official language logos below language names in the implementation diagram.
- A clearly labelled equal-weight vote scenario compares 4/6 support against different six-person groups; support sets are disjoint. This is an illustration of prerequisites, not an Ethereum incident, protocol reproduction, proof that conformance causes bugs, or a claim that different validator sets are inherently invalid. The real theorem handles differing sets with an adjusted bound.
- Use original project logos from official sources; retain their colors and record retrieval URLs in `figs/logo-sources.json`. Do not draw imitation logos or recolor originals.
- Preserve explanatory illustrations and prominent quantitative charts when restructuring. Text boxes or logo lists alone are not substitutes. Keep a visual inventory and reconnect each figure to its intended slide. If baked-in captions are inaccurate, update the illustration rather than silently omitting it. The main result uses a donut and a 57-cell distribution with 48 non-explicit and 9 disputed conditions; disputed does not mean explicit.
- Visual clarity and content explanation are separate requirements. Prefer actual downloaded illustrations from credible external sources to homemade boxes and stick figures; use large images, short labels, and clear main/supporting hierarchy. Preserve the quantitative chart rather than substituting decorative art. Explain general concepts before Ethereum-specific names; keep exact theorem syntax and detailed caveats secondary to plain-language conditions and guarantees.
- Active external illustrations are listed in `css2026-slides.json`; `figs/illustration-sources.json` records original files (including unused legacy choices), authors, source pages, licenses, and hashes. `python fetch_illustrations.py` downloads those five chosen assets via official sites. Do not use these assets for ML development or redistribute them as an illustration pack.
- `python figs/build_visuals.py` builds the logo architecture diagram, overlap illustration, and result chart from the original logo files and research data in `css2026-slides.json`. Legacy generated images may contain superseded explanations and must not be pasted without review.
- General-system examples may be hypothetical (Raft restart/vote persistence, FIX order replay/deduplication, POSIX atomicity/durability). Label them as illustrative scenarios; do not claim measured omissions or proven audit effectiveness for those systems.
- Research source of truth: adjacent `../css2026-paper/paper.md`. Preconditions come from Lean proofs, not EIPs. The measured result is 48 of 57 repeated conditions unanimously classified non-explicit in specification prose by automated annotators, corresponding to 7 of 10 concepts. It is not a vulnerability count. Same-domain bug evidence is not proof of causation. Downstream audit effectiveness is untested.
- Distinguish implementation obligations, environmental/threat-model assumptions, and proof auxiliary conditions. Do not convert every premise to an assertion or translate model-level slashing into completed real-world asset confiscation.
- `本研究の内容` states the research question and the work in two blocks, with one worked comparison inside the second: the Lean theorem from `gasper-lean4` beside the matching consensus-specs excerpt, highlighting the premises and the spec text they do or do not correspond to. The two slides after it are the inventories that make the counts checkable — `コンセンサスプロトコルの安全性に関する17の定理` (each theorem named with a one-line plain-language reading of its statement) and `それに対応するSPECのファイル` (the consensus-specs files and sections those theorems map to, with the per-section theorem count, over the 89 files searched at commit `2359a5e`). Keep the names and numbers tied to `gasper-lean4` at `7650709` and the paper's appendices. Do not put CHK-* identifiers on slides: they are internal plumbing and only confuse the audience. Both code panels quote real source; keep the spec panel verbatim and put findings in rows outside it, never as invented comment lines inside the quoted code. State the `quorum` zero-hit result together with the caveat that `weak-subjectivity.md` and `fast-confirmation.md` do describe safety consequences in prose; 非E means the decomposed premises are not given as checkable requirements, not that the concepts are absent.
- Use CRLF/LF-tolerant slide separators and `Note:` speaker notes. Never override Reveal.js section visibility, positioning, or transforms with forced display/top rules.
- Serve via HTTP: `python -m http.server 8791`. Verify actual browser rendering with `python verify_slides.py --capture`; checks all 28 slides at four viewport sizes, note hiding, images, navigation, and the speaker popup. Python Playwright and Pillow are available in the current environment; no repository dependency installation was needed.
- `python fetch_logos.py` retrieves the pinned official-site asset URLs and refreshes their provenance manifest.
- Deployment: `node build_site.mjs` stages `dist/` with only the files the deck renders — `index.html`, `slides.md`, the `figs/` assets referenced by those two, and the provenance manifests. `wrangler.jsonc` runs it through `build.command` and points Cloudflare Workers static assets at `dist/`, so `npx wrangler deploy` is self-contained and Workers Builds needs no build command of its own; a push to `main` deploys to https://css2026-slides.kingmasatojames.workers.dev. Keep the staging script dependency-free and in Node — the build image is not guaranteed to have Python. Never widen the asset directory to the repository root: `materials/` holds speaker crib notes and anticipated questions that must not be published.
